# Strategy — @inmigraforma

Repositioned 2026-07 from promoting immigration services to **US immigration
news, explained bilingually (Spanish-first)**. The account has a small real
audience (~160 followers) acquired under the old positioning — treat them as
Spanish-speaking people personally affected by US immigration policy.

## Working hypotheses (unproven)

- The existing audience stays and engages more with clear news explainers than
  with promotional content.
  *Evidence still missing: no post-repositioning snapshot exists. Needs ~6-8
  published carousels with saves/reach recorded before this can be judged.*
- Spanish-first explainers of official English-language sources fill a real
  gap: the primary sources are hard to read, and much Spanish-language
  coverage is alarmist. Calm + sourced is the differentiator.
  *Evidence still missing: no comparative data. We will never publish an
  alarmist variant, so this can only ever be supported indirectly — comment
  quality, saves, follower retention.*
- Saves should be the key metric: dates and rule changes are things people
  keep. Shares matter when a change affects a whole community.
  *Evidence still missing: zero recorded saves or shares. Treat "saves beat
  likes" as an assumption, not something we have observed.*
- Reels reach beyond the existing audience; carousels serve it. Alternate.
  *Evidence still missing: this batch is carousels only; no Reel has run under
  the new positioning, so there is no reach comparison at all.*

## Non-negotiables (not hypotheses)

- News comes ONLY from official government outlets (USCIS, DHS, State
  Department, Federal Register, EOIR). No secondary-press reporting as news.
- Immigration-lawyer commentary is allowed ONLY as clearly labelled opinion
  ("OPINIÓN", lawyer/firm named) with the not-legal-advice disclaimer.
- Every claim carries a source and a date on the slide itself.
- No individual legal advice, ever. The disclaimer line closes every caption.
- Never alarmist. People make life decisions on this information.
- Visual continuity: keep the account's existing style — no big changes in
  colors or fonts (owner's instruction, 2026-07-27).
- No caption may promise a reply, a DM, a template, a checklist or a document.
  Nobody watches the inbox. Save / share / opinion prompts only.

Deliberate choices, to be revisited once there is data:
- Four of seven posts are *method* posts (reading a Federal Register docket,
  spotting a later correction, how litigation surfaces in USCIS alerts, the
  five-source list). Rationale: they stay accurate well beyond two weeks and
  are the kind of thing people save. Completely untested.
- No OPINIÓN slide in this batch. A law-firm analysis of the asylum rule
  exists, but its substance could not be verified against the firm's own
  wording within the search budget, and paraphrasing commentary we have not
  actually read would breach the labelled-opinion rule. Carry the OPINIÓN
  format forward to a batch where the commentary can be cited accurately.
- The dollar amount of the H.R. 1 annual asylum fee was deliberately NOT put
  on any slide: not verified in an official source this batch. Slides send
  readers to uscis.gov (Fees). Do not fill this gap from memory next time —
  verify it or omit it again.
- The litigation post (post06) is framed around the durable ideas ("in part",
  "administrative stay = temporary", check the alerts page) rather than around
  the current scope, precisely because the scope can move within days.
